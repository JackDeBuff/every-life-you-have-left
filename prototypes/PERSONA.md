# Demo persona for the visual prototypes

Both prototypes (The Delta, Four Horizons) use this same made-up person, so the owner can compare
the two visual concepts fairly. The data is hand-written. No AI calls happen in the prototypes.

## Alex, 25, Durham NC (today = 2026)

- Born 2001. Raised by a single mom, who lives 20 minutes away.
- Moved to Durham in 2019 for college. Graduated in 2023. First job in 2024.
- Has been a data analyst at a regional bank for 2 years. Paints at night.
- Owes $28k in student loans.
- Has been offered a transfer to the Singapore office and hasn't answered yet.

### What Alex said (quote ids are cited by the "why" of each turning point)

| id | quote |
|---|---|
| q1 | "I can't say no to my mom." |
| q2 | "Money scares me. I still have $28k in loans." |
| q3 | "If nobody judged me, I'd go to art school and draw for a living." |
| q4 | "My biggest fear is waking up at 40 in the same cubicle." |
| q5 | "Singapore offered me a transfer and I haven't answered." |

### Base-rate facts to cite (one per turning point is enough)

- 1 in 4 US adults is a family caregiver (AARP 2025).
- Only about 35% of new businesses survive 10 years (BLS).
- People hold about 4.5 jobs between ages 25 and 34 (BLS NLSY).
- The median first-time home buyer is 40 (NAR 2025).
- Life satisfaction dips on average around age 48 (Blanchflower, 145 countries).
- Relationships at 50 predicted health at 80 better than cholesterol did (Harvard Study of Adult Development).

### Domain states per year (0–100)

Each life carries these states for every year: happiness, wealth, career, relationships (partner),
family (mom and origin), children, mental health, achievement, life goals, risk. Interpolate between
the keyframes below. Hard years show lower mental health. Risk spikes during leaps and layoffs.

## The four lives (2026 → 2071, age 25 → 70)

No death and no serious illness for Alex. Mom moving in to be cared for is fine. Divorce and layoffs
are fine.

### 1. Your Path ("if nothing changes", the one the pauses steer)
- 2029: turns down Singapore, because of q1.
- 2031: promoted to senior analyst.
- 2033: meets Sam. 2035: marries.
- 2037: first child.
- 2041: burnout year (q4). Murky, a heavy year.
- 2043: switches to teaching data at a community college.
- 2048: mom moves in (caregiving base rate).
- 2052: paints again on weekends.
- 2058: the child leaves home.
- 2066: retires.
- At 70: *"Steady, and quieter than you planned."*

### 2. The Leap
- 2027: says yes to Singapore (q5).
- 2028–29: lonely years.
- 2030: meets a partner in Singapore.
- 2032: laid off in a restructuring (base rate: about 4.5 jobs between 25 and 34).
- 2033: starts freelance illustration.
- 2036: moves to Lisbon.
- 2040: first show.
- 2045: opens a studio.
- No kids. Mom visits once a year.
- Money swings, then becomes comfortable.
- At 70: *"Louder than you planned."*

### 3. The Careful Life (fear wins)
- Stays at the bank. Pays off the debt by 2030 (q2).
- Climbs to VP in 2045. Ends with the most money of the four.
- 2039: the marriage ends.
- 2040–42: heavy years.
- 2050: reconnects with old friends.
- Never paints.
- At 70: *"Safe, and you know exactly what it cost."*

### 4. The Unjudged Life
- 2027: goes to art school (MFA) on loans (q3).
- 2027–34: money is tight.
- 2034: meets a partner.
- 2035: illustrates children's books.
- 2038: a child.
- Stays near mom, so both lives are kept.
- 2050: a book wins an award.
- At 70: *"Poorer on paper, richer every Tuesday."*

## The pause to prototype (1 is enough)

At **2031, age 30**, on Your Path:

> "It's 2031. You're 30. Your manager asks if you want to lead the new team, but it means
> weekends. Sam just asked you to take a painting class together. What do you protect first?"

Whatever the user types, Your Path re-routes from 2031 onward to a precomputed alternate. In the
alternate, Alex protects the painting: the 2041 burnout is softer, a 2044 first small exhibition is
added, and the 70 line becomes *"Steady, with color in it."* Show the change summary in one line.

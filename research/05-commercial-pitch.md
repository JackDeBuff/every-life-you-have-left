## 0. The answer in one screen

- **Lead with:** **students at their first crossroads**: college students and new grads, 18 and
  older, deciding what comes after graduation.
- **Who pays:** **university career centers** buy a campus license. Students use it free and spread
  it through share cards.
- **Track:** **Social Impact & Education.**
- **"And it scales to…":** **every crossroads after graduation.** The same engine, sold to whoever
  already pays to help adults through the next turn:
  - outplacement (a layoff)
  - employers' benefits and 401(k) enrollment (a first job)
  - midlife coaching (a reset)
  - retirement and protection providers (retirement)

  Banks and insurers are the big-money end of this story, not the opening line.
- **Why this wedge:**
  1. The demo audience (hackers and Duke judges) **is** the target user.
  2. The Saturday pilot runs on the **same population** as MIT's study (ages 18–30).
  3. A career center is a buyer we can **pilot in a semester**, without a compliance department.
  4. In the Social Impact track, a team with a **real payer** stands out, because most teams there
     have none.
  5. The money comes later, in the expansion.
- **Pitch motif:** **"Four lives, four crossroads."** Four lives is the product. Four crossroads
  (graduation, layoff, midlife, retirement) is the business.

## 2. Segment research

Each segment below covers who pays, the price model, the size of the market, competitors and
precedents, why now, and the regulatory risk. Scores are in section 3.

### A. University career centers and college students, 18+ (**recommended wedge**)
- **Who pays and who buys:**
  - The buyer is the career center director, an assistant dean of student success, or a
    first-generation or student-success program.
  - Students use it free and bring it to the career center themselves, the way Handshake and
    Grammarly spread on campuses.
- **Price (est.):**
  - Campus license of **$2–5 per enrolled student per year**, which is about **$10k–$40k per
    campus**.
  - Start with a free or $5k pilot for one graduating class.
  - Benchmark: K-12 career-readiness software sold to Arkansas districts in 2019–20 for
    **$3.50–$8.50 per student per year** (Naviance packages)
    ([Arkansas DESE chart](https://dese.ade.arkansas.gov/Files/20210104111006_CCR_chart.pdf)).
    Handshake's university pricing is private.
- **Size of the market:**
  - **19.4M** US postsecondary students in fall 2025: 16.2M undergraduates and 3.2M graduate
    students ([National Student Clearinghouse](https://nscresearchcenter.org/final-fall-enrollment-trends/)).
  - At $3 per student, the campus layer alone is about **$58M a year (est.)**. That is a real but
    modest market.
  - **Say it honestly:** campuses are where we earn trust and proof, not where we stop.
- **The pain (good stage numbers):**
  - **About 1 in 6** US grads say career services was "very helpful," and just as many say "not at
    all helpful" ([Gallup 2016](https://news.gallup.com/poll/199307/one-six-grads-say-career-services-helpful.aspx)).
  - **33%** of bachelor's students change major within 3 years
    ([NCES 2018-434](https://nces.ed.gov/pubs2018/2018434/)).
  - Only **56%** of Gen Z feel prepared for what lies ahead
    ([Gallup/Walton Family Foundation 2025](https://nextgeninsights.waltonfamilyfoundation.org/resources/2025-voices-of-gen-z-study/)).
  - Upstream, in high schools: **376 students per school counselor** in 2023–24, against a
    recommended 250 ([ASCA](https://schoolcounselor.org/getmedia/1282baef-9128-44cf-8b4b-63fa5cfda2b6/pr_ratios-23-24.pdf)).
    This is a high school figure, not a college one.
- **Competitors and precedents:**
  - Handshake does jobs, and VMock and Big Interview do résumés and interviews. All of them work
    on "get a job," not "which life."
  - MIT *Future You* is research, not a product.
  - Nobody sells **personal life simulations** to career centers.
- **Why they would buy now:** AI anxiety among grads, career centers measured on "first
  destination" outcomes, and a demand for tools that scale beyond appointment-based counseling.
- **Regulatory:**
  - **FERPA** applies only if the school shares education records with us. Students using it
    voluntarily, with no records from the school, keeps us mostly outside FERPA.
  - Campuses will still require a data-protection agreement and accessibility (VPAT/WCAG).
  - COPPA does not apply (18+).
  - The FTC Act covers efficacy claims: say *"built on research showing…"*, never *"our app
    reduces anxiety"* until we have our own data.
- **Risk:** small budgets and slow academic procurement (fiscal-year cycles). Pilots can start
  mid-year from a director's discretionary budget, which is typical but **unverified**.

### B. K-12 schools: "kids with no dream" (owner idea 2)
- **Who pays:** districts, out of CTE (Perkins) or college-and-career-readiness budgets. The
  counselor is the user, and the district CCR coordinator is the buyer.
- **Price:** $3.50–$8.50 per student per year (the Arkansas benchmark above).
- **Market and competitors:**
  - Crowded, with well-funded incumbents: **Naviance** (PowerSchool), **Xello**, **SchooLinks**
    (**$80M Series B**, Oct 2024
    ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241020113677/en/SchooLinks-Announces-%2480M-Series-B-Funding-Round-Led-by-Susquehanna-Growth-Equity-SGE))),
    YouScience, MajorClarity, and the free BigFuture.
  - We would be a feature they could add on, not a platform.
- **Pain:** the 376:1 counselor ratio. In Arizona it is **645:1**.
- **Regulatory (heavy):**
  - **COPPA** for anyone under 13. The amended rule took effect June 23, 2025, and the
    **compliance deadline passed April 22, 2026**
    ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule)).
  - **FERPA** applies, plus state student-privacy laws (SOPIPA-style) that cover K-12 vendors.
- **Ethics:** showing a 15-year-old from a poor zip code the "base rate" of their future can be
  read as **determinism**, as if the tool were telling them they will stay poor.
- **Verdict:** a powerful story and a bad wedge. Name it as the expansion **"down"** into high
  school seniors (18+ first), after the honesty and safety design has been proven with adults.

### C. College-access and mentoring nonprofits (College Advising Corps, Big Brothers Big Sisters)
- **Who pays:** grants and foundations, sometimes AmeriCorps-linked funding (volatile since 2025:
  **unverified**, check before citing).
- **Price:** program license of $5k–$50k (est.).
- **Fit:** a perfect story for the Social Impact track, with mentors using it as a conversation
  starter.
- **Risk:** slow, grant-dependent revenue.
- **Verdict:** great **pilot partners and case studies**, not a revenue engine. Mention one by
  name as a "who we'd pilot with."

### D. Banks, insurers, wealth managers, 401(k) recordkeepers (owner idea 1)
- **Who pays:** marketing, digital engagement, or retirement-participant engagement budgets.
  Models are per-engaged-user, per-lead, or enterprise license.
- **Size (the biggest dollars):**
  - **~100M** US adults say they need (more) life insurance; the need gap is 40% in 2025
    ([LIMRA/Life Happens 2025 Barometer](https://lifehappens.org/press/adults-age-30-and-younger-overestimate-life-insurance-cost-by-10-12-times/),
    [LIMRA series](https://www.limra.com/en/research/research-series/2025-insurance-barometer-series/)).
  - About three-quarters of adults overestimate what life insurance costs.
  - Only **35%** of non-retirees think their retirement savings is on track, and only **22% of
    18–29-year-olds**. Only 38% of 18–29-year-olds have a 401(k) or IRA at all
    ([Fed SHED, data for 2025](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2026-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2025-savings-investments.htm)).
  - The "about 1 in 4 non-retirees have no retirement savings at all" figure appears in earlier
    SHED years ([PlanSponsor](https://www.planadviser.com/?p=55937)). It is **unverified for the
    latest year**, so prefer the "on track" figures.
  - 401(k)s: **~70M active participants, $10.1T** at the end of 2025
    ([ICI](https://www.ici.org/resource-hubs/401k)).
- **Precedents (strong):**
  - **Merrill Edge "Face Retirement"** launched in **December 2012**. It aged your webcam photo
    to push saving and had ~350k visitors soon after launch
    ([BusinessWire](https://businesswire.com/news/home/20121204005580/en/Merrill-Edge%C2%AE-Launches-Face-Retirement),
    [Financial Planning](https://www.financial-planning.com/news/a-look-into-the-future-helps-investors-save-for-retirement)).
  - **Aegon UK "Postcards from Tomorrow"** (Mylo): an AI-generated image of your future self for
    pension engagement, built on Google Cloud
    ([Waracle](https://waracle.com/insights/artificial-intelligence/reimagining-pension-engagement-with-ai-and-google-cloud-at-aegon/)).
  - The science: Hershfield et al. 2011 (section 7).
- **Why they would buy now:** Gen Z and Millennials are the least covered and the hardest to
  engage. Insurers want "pre-aware to ready" journeys
  ([LOMA](https://www.loma.org/en/news/marketfacts/2025/from-preaware-to-ready-what-is-important-to-consumers)).
- **Regulatory (heavy):**
  - **FINRA Rule 2210(d)(1)(F)** has historically barred projections of performance. Interactive
    "likelihood" tools must meet **Rule 2214**: FINRA access, disclosures, and suitability still
    apply ([FINRA 2214](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/2214)).
  - The **SEC approved FINRA amendments permitting projections and targeted returns on Oct 15,
    2026**, with written policies and procedures, and the effective date is still pending
    ([Troutman alert, Oct 27 2026](https://www.troutman.com/insights/sec-approves-finra-rule-2210-amendments-permitting-projections-and-targeted-returns-broader-rule-2210-modernization-proposal-pending/)).
    Reported, not confirmed against the SEC release.
  - RIAs have the SEC Marketing Rule (hypothetical performance).
  - Insurers face state unfair-trade-practice and illustration rules, and NAIC's AI model bulletin
    (**unverified** for scope).
  - **Our stance:** we never project investment returns or recommend products. We simulate life
    events with public base rates. The partner's compliance team owns anything product-specific.
- **Risks:** 9–18 month enterprise sales (est.), compliance review, and **the optics of fear
  selling** (section 9).
- **Verdict:** the **biggest money and the best precedent**, but the **worst wedge for a weekend**.
  It is the climax of the expansion story.

### E. Financial advisors and planners (an SMB tool for client meetings)
- **Use:** the "discovery meeting." The client plays out four lives with the advisor, and goals
  come out of it in the client's own words.
- **Price:** $49–$149 per advisor per month (est.).
  - Planning software benchmarks: RightCapital **$139.95–$254.95 per advisor per month**
    ([RightCapital pricing](https://rightcapital.com/pricing)).
  - eMoney is about $379 per month (**unverified**, third-party).
- **Speed:** fast. Independent RIAs buy with a credit card.
- **Risks:** firm compliance review of client-facing tools, and a crowded advisor-tech stack.
- **Verdict:** **the fastest B2B revenue in finance.** A good second step after campuses, and a
  low-risk way into the finance story.

### F. Employers: HR, L&D, benefits, financial-wellness platforms
- **Size:**
  - US corporate training spend is **$102.8B** (2025) ([Training magazine](https://trainingmag.com/2025-training-industry-report)).
  - Financial-wellness benefits are about **$2.5B globally** (2024, est. by
    [DataHorizzon](https://datahorizzonresearch.com/financial-wellness-benefits-market-39789)).
    The US alone is about $0.6B (2023) rising to $1.2B by 2029
    ([Arizton](https://www.arizton.com/market-reports/us-financial-wellness-benefits-market/description)).
    These are vendor estimates and definitions vary.
- **Pain:** **59%** of employees are stressed about their finances (PwC 2026 survey, ~3,500
  employees) ([PwC](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/services/consulting/human-resources/library/employee-financial-wellness-survey.html),
  [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/financial-stress-drags-employee-engagement-down/818415/)).
- **Channel:** sell through benefits platforms and financial-wellness vendors as an "engagement
  moment" at open enrollment or 401(k) enrollment.
- **Risks:** the employer must **never** see an individual's futures (that would be surveillance),
  and benefits sales cycles are slow.
- **Verdict:** strong expansion, slow start.

### G. Outplacement and laid-off workers (the next crossroads)
- **Who pays:** the employer's HR, per laid-off employee, through outplacement firms (LHH,
  Careerminds, RiseSmart and others).
- **Size:** about **$5.2B globally in 2025** ([Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/outplacement-market)).
  Other estimates run $2–7B, so treat the range as rough.
- **Fit:** excellent. A layoff is exactly a "four lives" moment, the buyer already pays per head,
  and the users are adults.
- **Price:** per seat, $25–$100 (est.), or a platform license to an outplacement firm.
- **Verdict:** **the first expansion.** It is the most natural "next crossroads."

### H. Midlife reset (coaching, therapy-adjacent) (owner idea 3)
- **Size:**
  - US life coaching is about **$2.6B** (2026) ([IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/industry/life-coaches/4184/)).
  - US job training and career counseling is about **$17–20B** (IBISWorld revisions conflict)
    ([IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/industry-statistics/market-size/job-training-career-counseling-united-states)).
- **Pain:**
  - Well-being bottoms out around a median age of **47** across 132 countries (Blanchflower,
    NBER) ([coverage](https://www.foxnews.com/lifestyle/mid-life-crisis-may-be-culmination-of-lifes-peak-of-misery-study-reveals)).
  - About **24%** of older US workers planned a job change in 2025 (AARP, via an aggregator:
    **unverified**).
- **Channel:** coaches buy it as a session tool for $30–100 a month (est.), or sell it B2C.
- **Risks:**
  - It pulls you into the **Health & Wellness** track and its clinical-safety questions.
  - Last year's wellness and reflection apps all lost.
  - Vulnerable users (depression).
- **Verdict:** the third crossroads in the expansion. **Do not lead with it.**

### I. Military transition (TAP / SkillBridge)
- **Size:** over **200,000** service members separate every year, and TAP is mandatory for anyone
  with 180+ days of active duty ([CRS R48114](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R48114.html)).
- **Fit:** strong story (identity loss, "what life next").
- **Risk:** government procurement is slow. Go through veteran nonprofits first.
- **Verdict:** a social-impact expansion. Good for Q&A ("who else needs this?").

### J. Re-entry and recovery programs
- **Evidence:**
  - High-school youth who "befriended" a future-self avatar reported **less delinquency**
    (van Gelder et al., *Criminology* 2015).
  - Convicted offenders who interacted with their future self in VR showed **less self-defeating
    behavior** (alcohol, overspending) a week later, in a small sample
    ([Sci Reports 2022](https://doaj.org/article/b36c017ed1cd4689954aef76b85cd1c4)).
- **Who pays:** grant-funded, with slow revenue.
- **Verdict:** a powerful proof point for "the science works on hard populations," but not a
  market.

### K. B2C freemium and share-card virality
- **Precedents:**
  - *1 in 8 Billion* (viral, Oct 2026).
  - **BitLife**: players lived **72M+ virtual lives in one year**
    ([Goodgame](https://goodgamestudios.com/?p=65830)), and US weekly active users peaked at
    about 2.5M in Q1 2024 ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/2024-q1-android-top-5-simulator%20games-revenue-us-60416e41241bc16eb8905c2f)).
  - Tomo's paid subscriptions.
- **Model:** free first run; then $4.99 to re-run, save, or keep chatting, or $7.99 a month for
  "future self that stays with you" (the parked idea).
- **Risks:**
  - One-shot novelty (weak retention).
  - LLM cost per run: **measure it on Saturday**, as tokens per run × price.
  - High consumer customer-acquisition cost if there is no virality.
- **Verdict:** the **distribution engine, not the business model**. Share cards are how students
  bring it to their campus.

### L. Licensing the "future self engine" as an API
- **Verdict:** premature. Mention it only as the long-term platform ("anyone who needs a person
  to feel their future").

### M. Couples and pre-marriage counseling (parked; could feed fromyourlove)
- **Verdict:** keep it parked. If asked: "a 'future us' mode is on the roadmap."

## 4. Recommendation: wedge, expansion, track

### The wedge
**Free for every student. Licensed by the career centers who are supposed to help them picture
their future.**
- **First 90 days, said on stage as the plan:**
  1. Pilot with one graduating class at one campus. Try Duke first, since the hackathon is a warm
     intro.
  2. Measure future-self connection and "made a concrete plan" before and after.
  3. Turn the result into the case study that sells campus number two.

### The expansion: "four lives, four crossroads"
| Crossroads | Who already pays to help | Why they'd pay |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Graduation** (wedge) | University career centers, college-access programs | Students can't picture any life past graduation |
| **2. A layoff** | Employers, through outplacement firms (~$5.2B market) | Laid-off workers have to pick a new path fast |
| **3. Midlife reset** | Coaches, employers' L&D and benefits | Well-being bottoms out around 47 |
| **4. Retirement and protection** | 401(k) providers (70M participants), insurers (100M underinsured), advisors | People don't save for a stranger, and their future self *is* a stranger |

> Spoken version: *"We start where everyone's first crossroads is: graduation. But the engine
> works at every crossroads after it, and each one already has someone who pays to help: career
> centers, outplacement firms, employers, and the retirement industry. Merrill Lynch and Aegon have
> already paid for crude versions of this, with an aged photo. We're the honest one."*

### The track: **Social Impact & Education**
| Track | Fit | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Social Impact & Education** (pick) | Students at a crossroads, counselor shortage, an honest tool for people who never got told what they could become | Competing with tutoring apps. We stand out on visuals and a real business model |
| Finance / Enterprise / Tech | Banks, 401(k), insurers | The demo isn't about money. Compliance and manipulation questions. Up against hard enterprise tools (last year: *Case Closed*) |
| Health & Wellness | Anxiety reduction (MIT), midlife | Clinical-claims and safety questions you can't answer in a weekend. Last year's wellness apps all lost |
| Entertainment / Media / Arts | Game-like story view, *1 in 8 Billion* | Weaker commercial-viability story, and "a game" undersells it |

## 5. The pitch

The owner is 25: *"I have 45 summers left."* The app should compute summers as 70 minus age.

### (a) 30-second elevator pitch (about 85 words; read it aloud, it should take about 30 seconds)

> "Your brain thinks about your future self the way it thinks about a stranger, and people don't
> plan for strangers. **Every Life You Have Left** plays out four lives you could still live, year
> by year, from what you tell it: the life you're on, the leap, the one where fear wins, and the one
> you secretly want. Every turning point is traced to your own words and a real-world base rate, so
> it's not a horoscope. Students use it free; career centers license it. Give me 90 seconds and
> I'll show you yours."

The "stranger" line comes from Hershfield's fMRI work (section 7).

### (b) The 90-second expo-table version (the judge becomes the demo)

**Setup at the table:**
- A laptop facing the judge, and a **QR code stand** so the second judge runs it on their own
  phone at the same time.
- A **pre-generated "owner's life"** tab as a fallback.
- A **fast path**: age, one line about yourself, then 3 open questions, spoken or typed.
- **Start generating after the first answer** and stream the rest in.

| Time | What the owner says (exact lines) | What is on screen |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | "Hi! Give me 90 seconds and I'll show you four of the lives you have left." | Title, plus "How old are you, and what do you do? One line." |
| 0:05 | "Just your age and one line. Say it out loud if that's faster." | Judge types or speaks, e.g. "45, I run a lab at Duke." |
| 0:12 | "Your past fills in from what you just told me. That marker is today. Only the future can be played." | Years 0–45 fill fast; **YOU ARE HERE**; "25 summers left." |
| 0:18 | (reads the first question aloud) "What would you do if nobody judged you?" | Q1 (open text or mic). Generation starts in the background |
| 0:28 | "Who do you find it hardest to say no to?" | Q2 |
| 0:36 | "What are you most afraid of losing?" | Q3 |
| 0:45 | "Here are four of you, starting from today. The one you're on. The leap. The one where fear wins. And the one you secretly want." | Four lives play side by side, years ticking, with meters |
| 0:58 | (tap a turning point on their life) "Look: 2031, you turn down the chair role. Why? Because you told me *'the lab is the only place I feel like myself.'* And here's the real base rate behind it." | A **why card**: their quote highlighted, a statistic with its source |

### (e) The hardest judge questions, with crisp answers

**1. "Isn't this just ChatGPT with a prompt?"**
> "Ask ChatGPT about your future and you get one flattering story in a chat bubble. This is a
> simulation: four lives held consistent for 45 years, built on real base rates, where every turning
> point links back to the sentence you said. Tap any year and it shows you why. And it re-steers
> when you answer mid-life. [If the control was run:] We tested it yesterday against plain ChatGPT:
> ours moved connection by [X], ChatGPT by [Y]."

**2. "How is this not a horoscope?"**
> "Three ways. Every event is traced to your own words. Every turning point cites a real base rate,
> like how often people in your field move abroad or change careers. And we don't claim to predict
> anything. The research says the benefit comes from making your future vivid and connected, not
> from being right. We're making it vivid enough to act on."

**3. "What happens to my data?"**
> "Your data stays in your browser. It goes to the model only to generate your lives, and we don't
> store it or train on it. We never ask your race. LinkedIn comes in as your own PDF, and we never
> scrape. For schools, the student owns the result, and the institution sees only anonymous, opt-in
> totals, never anyone's lives."

Say only what the build actually does. If uploads go to a server, say so.

**4. "Couldn't this hurt someone who's already struggling?"**
> "We designed for that. There's no death and no serious illness, and every life stops at 70. The
> 'fear wins' life always shows the moment it could have turned. If someone writes something
> worrying, we pause and show the 988 crisis line. Base rates shape the odds of events, but they
> never cap what's possible in 'the life you secretly want.' And we start with adults only."

**5. "Who pays, and why would they?"**
> "Students use it free, and that's the growth engine: share cards. Career centers pay about $3 per
> student, because they're judged on student outcomes and can't give every student an hour. Then
> the same engine goes to every crossroads that already has a payer: outplacement, employers,
> 401(k) providers. Merrill Lynch and Aegon have already paid for aged-photo versions. Tomo just
> raised $5M from Bain on 'future self' alone."

**6. "What's the moat? Anyone can call an LLM."**
> "Short term, it's craft: the simulation, the tracing and the story view are hard to make feel
> real, which is why nobody has shipped it. Long term, it's three things: a **calibration loop**
> (every opt-in 'did this feel like me?' rating teaches the model what believable looks like), the
> **outcome evidence** institutions need before they buy, and **distribution** through campuses."

The calibration loop conflicts with "never stored." The owner must decide (section 10).

**7. "Does it actually change behavior, or just feelings?"**
> "Honestly, the immediate effect on connection and anxiety is well shown; MIT measured it.
> Behavior change in the field is real but modest. That's why we built pauses and chat, not a
> one-time video, and why our customers measure outcomes like 'made a concrete plan.'"

**8. "Why a solo builder?"** (if asked)
> "I build animated story products for a living. My site has paying customers. Taste and motion are
> my edge, and that's what makes a simulation feel real."

### (f) The number to bring: Saturday pilot, 30 minutes, one Google Form

**Goal:** a before-and-after **future self-connection** score on 10–15 fellow hackers.
Ideally also a **plain ChatGPT control**, which answers question 1 with data.

**One form, three sections**, so each person's before and after answers stay paired:
- **Section 1, before (60 seconds):**
  1. Age (number).
  2. **Overlapping circles** (Ersner-Hershfield et al. 2009 single-item measure): "Which pair of
     circles best describes how connected you feel to *yourself at 70*?" Seven images, from
     not overlapping to almost fully overlapping, scored 1–7. Draw the 7 pairs in any tool and
     paste them as image choices.
  3. "How **vividly** can you picture your life at 70?" (1–7)
  4. "How **similar** do you feel to the person you'll be at 70?" (1–7)
  5. "How **anxious** do you feel about your future right now?" (1–7)

  Items 3–5 are adapted from the subscales of the Future Self-Continuity Questionnaire (Sokol &
  Serper 2020) used in the MIT study. Label them "adapted."
- **Section 2, the intervention:**
  - "Open the link and do the whole thing. Come back to this tab when you're done." Put the app
    link and a QR code here.
  - **Control (coin flip, about 1 in 3 people):** a different section that says "Open ChatGPT and
    type: *'I'm [age], I [one line]. Describe what my life might look like at 70.'* Read it for
    3 minutes, then come back." Use Google Forms' "go to section based on answer" with a heads or
    tails question.
- **Section 3, after:**
  - Repeat items 2–5.
  - "How realistic did your futures feel?" (1–7)
  - "Which turning point surprised you most?" (open)
  - "Is there one thing you'll do differently this week? What?" (open; this is behavioral intent)
  - "Would you share your card?" (Y/N)
  - "Would you want your career center to offer this?" (Y/N)
  - Rate it 0–10.

**How to run it in 30 minutes:**
- Go to the **Saturday dinner tables**, which is where people sit still.
- Everyone runs it **on their own phone at the same time** (the fast path takes about 3–5
  minutes).
- Say: "Help me with a 5-minute test of my hack, anonymous."
- **Ask for no names.** Call it a "user test" or "pilot," never a "study." Exclude anyone under 18.

**Analysis in Google Sheets (5 minutes):**
- Mean before, mean after, and the change for each item.
- How many of N went up.
- A paired t-test: `=T.TEST(B2:B15, F2:F15, 2, 1)`.
- If the control has 5 or more people, compare the two changes. Do not claim significance with
  tiny samples.
- Say: **"pilot, N = [n], no control"** or **"…vs ChatGPT, N = [m]"**.

**Template line for the slide (fill in only real numbers; never invent them):**
> "[11] of [14] hackers felt more connected to their 70-year-old self. Average connection went from
> [3.1] to [4.6] out of 7. Plain ChatGPT: [3.2] to [3.5]."

For reference only (different measures, so **do not compare directly**): MIT's Future You
condition raised its future self-continuity composite by **+0.42 on a 7-point scale**, against
**0.00** for control and **+0.06** for a generic chatbot.

**Two bonus quotes:** take the best two answers to "one thing you'll do differently" (with the
person's OK) and put them on the Devpost page and the stage slide.

## 7. The science: what's safe to say

### Verified this session
- **MIT *Future You* (2024).**
  - Design: preregistered, IRB-approved, **N = 344** Prolific participants aged 18–30, four
    conditions (Future You 73, Questionnaire 76, generic Chat 103, Control 92). The AI future self
    was built from a survey and the chat lasted 10–30 minutes.
  - **Future self-continuity change:** +0.42 (SD 0.70) for Future You, against 0.00 for control,
    +0.06 for the generic chatbot, and +0.22 for the questionnaire (p < .001).
  - **Anxiety change:** −0.68, against +0.21 for control (p = .001).
  - Negative emotion and lack of motivation also fell.
  - **Not significant:** agency, optimism, future consideration, self-esteem.
  - **Measured only right after the session.**
  - Sources: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12514),
    [MIT](https://www.media.mit.edu/publications/future-you-a-conversation-with-an-ai-generated-future-self-reduces-anxiety-negative-emotions-and-increases-future-self-continuity/).
- **Hershfield et al. 2011** (*Journal of Marketing Research*).
  - People who saw age-progressed avatars of themselves allocated **6.17% vs 4.41%** of pay to
    retirement.
  - This was a **hypothetical** allocation task
    ([DOL CLEAR review](https://clear.dol.gov/study/increasing-saving-behavior-through-age-progressed-renderings-future-self-hershfield-et-al-2011)).
  - NYU's "about 33% more" press figure doesn't match the paper's numbers, so **don't use "33%"**.
- **Field evidence is mixed and modest.**
  - A Chilean investment-firm field experiment found only a short-term, modest effect on money
    invested (Kausel et al. 2024)
    ([record](https://repositorio-az.uc.cl/dspace/items/35a72ddc-3c3a-4440-93aa-9b3c8b9ed9fd)).
  - A Mexican pension field trial (about 49k clients) moved one-time contributions but not
    recurring ones ([Behavioral Science & Policy, 2023](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/23794607231190607)).
  - A text-based "aging prime" replication found no effect (Stockdale & Sanders 2020).
  - **This is why we say "connection," not "proven to change behavior."**
- **van Gelder, Hershfield & Nordgren 2013** (*Psychological Science*). Students who met their aged
  self in VR cheated less: **6% vs 23.5%**
  ([Kellogg Insight](https://insight.kellogg.northwestern.edu/article/for_long_range_thinking_imagine_the_future_self)).
- **Reiff, Hershfield & Quoidbach 2020.** In 4,963 people, feeling more similar to your future self
  predicted **higher life satisfaction 10 years later**
  ([record](https://merit.url.edu/ca/publications/identity-over-time-perceived-similarity-between-selves-predicts-w-3/)).
  A later reanalysis raised a question about the method.

### From memory, check before saying
- **The "future self as a stranger" brain finding** (Ersner-Hershfield et al. 2009, *Social
  Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience*). Brain activity when thinking about your future self looks
  more like thinking about a stranger, and people with more similar activity discounted the
  future less.
  - It is widely cited (Hershfield's TED talk and his book *Your Future Self*, 2023).
  - The **overlapping circles** item is from Ersner-Hershfield et al. 2009 (*Judgment and Decision
    Making*).

### Say it on stage (safe)
- "MIT's 2024 study of 344 young adults: a short chat with an AI future self reduced anxiety and
  increased connection to the future self."
- "People who see their future selves put more of their pay toward retirement, in Hershfield's
  experiments."
- "376 students per school counselor" (ASCA 2023–24). "1 in 6 grads say career services was very
  helpful" (Gallup).
- "100 million Americans say they need more life insurance" (LIMRA 2025). "Only 22% of adults
  under 30 think their retirement savings is on track" (Federal Reserve).
- "Merrill Lynch built an aged-photo retirement tool in 2012. Aegon built an AI one."

### Don't say
- "It predicts your future." / "Proven to change behavior." / "33% more savings." /
  "Millions played *1 in 8 Billion*." / "It reduces anxiety" (as a claim about *our* product,
  until our own data shows it).

## 9. Ethics positioning: how a bank can use this without it being manipulative

**The risk in one sentence:** "A bank uses your scariest future to sell you insurance" sounds like
a dark pattern, and judges will think it even if they don't say it.

**The reframe:** *"Merrill showed people their wrinkles. We show people their choices, and they
own the result."* We are the **honest** layer between a person and any institution that wants
them to plan.

### The Honest Futures Charter (put it on the stage slide and the Devpost page)
1. **Nobody can pay to change your future.**
   - Sponsors and partners can't buy events, outcomes, or product placement inside the
     simulation.
   - There is never a "life where you bought our policy."
2. **Every number is public and cited.** Base rates come from public data, and the same engine runs
   for every partner.
3. **You own your futures.**
   - Data stays in your browser by default, with export and delete.
   - Institutions see only anonymous, opt-in totals, never an individual's lives.
   - It is never used to make decisions about you: not admissions, not hiring, not underwriting,
     not pricing.
   - This also keeps us clear of "consequential decision" AI laws such as Colorado's (delayed to
     June 30, 2026, current status **unverified**
     ([GT Law](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2025/9/colorado-delays-comprehensive-ai-law-with-further-changes-anticipated))).
4. **No fear selling.**
   - No death, no serious illness, no countdown pressure.
   - Every hard life shows the moment it could turn.
   - Partner offers never appear *inside* the story. The next step is something the user chooses
     ("take this to my counselor or advisor") and leads to a **human**, not a checkout.
5. **Measured on the user's outcome.** Success means connection, a concrete plan, or a savings rate,
   not partner conversion.

### Specific lines for the bank and insurer question
- *"A bank would license this the way banks fund EVERFI's financial-literacy courses: to make people
  more capable, not to sell inside the lesson."*
  - EVERFI was bought by Blackbaud for **~$750M** in 2021
    ([Techzine](https://www.techzine.eu/news/cloud/70583/blackbaud-acquires-everfi-for-750-million)).
    The detail that sponsors fund it so schools get it free is from memory and **unverified**.
- *"We don't project investment returns and we don't recommend products. That's the partner's
  licensed advisor, under their compliance rules."*
- *"Young adults overestimate the cost of life insurance by up to 10–12 times
  ([Life Happens](https://lifehappens.org/press/adults-age-30-and-younger-overestimate-life-insurance-cost-by-10-12-times/)).
  Honest information isn't manipulation. Hiding it is."*
